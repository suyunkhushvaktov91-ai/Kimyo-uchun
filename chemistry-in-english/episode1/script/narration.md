## Scene 1 — Hook: What is everything made of?

> Atom Studio: press `1`.
> **[1.0]** Title card.

Hello, and welcome to Chemistry in English. This is episode one.

In this series, we learn chemistry and English at the same time. Every episode brings new science ideas and new words.

> **[1.1]** The big question.

Look around you. Your phone. Your desk. The water in your glass. The air you breathe. Even your own body.

What are all these things made of?

Today we will answer this question. And on the way, we will learn twelve important chemistry words in English.

> **[1.2]** Vocabulary card: matter.

Here is our first word: **matter**.

Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. A stone is matter. Water is matter. Air is matter too, even though you cannot see it.

> **[1.3]** Zoom tunnel.

So, what is matter made of? Let's zoom in. And in. And in.

## Scene 2 — How small is an atom?

> Atom Studio: press `2`.
> **[2.0]** One human hair.

Take one hair from your head. It looks very thin, right? It is only about one tenth of a millimetre wide.

Now imagine that you cut something into smaller and smaller pieces. Again and again. Finally, you reach pieces so small that you cannot see them, even with a normal microscope.

> **[2.1]** Zoom into the hair: a wall of atoms.

Here they are. About one million of them could stand side by side across the width of that one hair. One million!

> **[2.2]** Vocabulary card: atom.

Each tiny piece is called an **atom**. An atom is a very small particle, and all matter is made of atoms. Your phone, the water, the air, and you.

> **[2.3]** "atomos" — the atom opens.

The word "atom" comes from the old Greek word "atomos". It means "cannot be cut". For a long time, people believed that atoms were the smallest things in the world.

Today we know more. We can look inside an atom. So let's do it.

---

## Scene 3 — Inside the atom

> Atom Studio: press `3`.
> **[3.0]** Vocabulary card: nucleus.

In the centre of every atom there is a **nucleus**. The nucleus is very, very small. But it is also very heavy.

> **[3.1]** Vocabulary card: mass. Mass bar appears.

In fact, almost all the mass of the atom is in the nucleus. That brings us to our next word: **mass**. Mass is the amount of matter in an object. We measure mass in grams and kilograms.

> **[3.2]** Vocabulary card: proton. Protons glow.

The nucleus contains two kinds of particles. The first one is the **proton**. A proton has a positive charge. We show it with a plus sign.

> **[3.3]** Vocabulary card: neutron. Neutrons glow.

The second one is the **neutron**. A neutron has no charge at all. It is neutral. Neutron, neutral. That is an easy way to remember it.

> **[3.4]** Vocabulary card: electron. Electrons start to move.

Now look around the nucleus. These very small, fast particles are **electrons**. An electron has a negative charge. We show it with a minus sign.

Electrons are very light. One electron is almost two thousand times lighter than one proton.

> **[3.5]** Vocabulary card: charge. Attraction lines.

We have used the word "charge" a few times, so let's make it our next word. **Charge** is a property of a particle. It can be positive, negative, or zero.

And here is the important rule: opposite charges attract. The positive protons pull the negative electrons. That is why the electrons stay close to the nucleus.

> **[3.6]** The stadium comparison.

And here is a surprising fact. Most of an atom is empty space.

Imagine an atom as big as a football stadium. The nucleus would be like a small pea in the middle of the field. And the electrons would be somewhere far away, around the seats at the top.

## Scene 4 — Elements and the atomic number

> Atom Studio: press `4`.
> **[4.0]** Vocabulary card: atomic number. The protons are counted.

Now, here is the most important idea of today. Count the protons in the nucleus. One, two, three, four, five, six.

This number is called the **atomic number**. This atom has six protons, so its atomic number is six.

> **[4.1]** Vocabulary card: element.

Why is this number so important? Because it tells us which **element** the atom is. An element is a pure substance made of only one kind of atom.

For example, a diamond is made of carbon atoms only. So carbon is an element.

> **[4.2]** Hydrogen.

Let's meet some elements. One proton? That is hydrogen. Atomic number one. It is the lightest element.

> **[4.3]** Helium.

Two protons? That is helium. Atomic number two. It is the gas inside party balloons that float up into the sky.

> **[4.4]** Carbon.

Six protons? That is carbon. Atomic number six. Carbon is in every living thing.

> **[4.5]** Oxygen.

Eight protons? That is oxygen. Atomic number eight. You are breathing it right now.

> **[4.6]** One proton flies into carbon — it becomes nitrogen.

Watch what happens when we add just one proton to carbon. Six protons become seven. And now it is not carbon any more. It is nitrogen, a completely different element.

One proton changes everything.

> **[4.7]** All 118 elements fill the periodic table.

Scientists know one hundred and eighteen elements today. Everything in the universe is built from them. Just like letters make all the words in a language, elements make all the matter around us.

## Scene 5 — Electron shells

> Atom Studio: press `5`.
> **[5.0]** Vocabulary card: shell. Empty shells.

Let's go back to the electrons. They do not fly around the nucleus randomly. They move in layers. We call these layers **shells**.

In our simple model, the first shell can hold up to two electrons. The second shell can hold up to eight.

> **[5.1]** Hydrogen: 1.

Let's build some atoms. Hydrogen has one electron. It goes into the first shell.

> **[5.2]** Helium: 2 — first shell full.

Helium has two electrons. Now the first shell is full.

> **[5.3]** Carbon: 2, 4.

Carbon has six electrons. Two in the first shell, and four in the second shell. We write this as two, four.

> **[5.4]** Oxygen: 2, 6.

Oxygen has eight electrons. Two, and six.

> **[5.5]** Neon: 2, 8 — both shells full.

Neon has ten electrons. Two, and eight. Now both shells are full.

Atoms with full shells are very stable. They do not like to react with other atoms. That is why neon is so calm.

> **[5.6]** Balance: +6 and −6.

Did you notice something? In every atom we built, the number of electrons is the same as the number of protons.

Carbon has six protons and six electrons. Six plus and six minus. They balance each other. So the whole atom has no charge.

## Scene 6 — Molecules, review and goodbye

> Atom Studio: press `6`.
> **[6.0]** Vocabulary card: molecule. Three atoms move together.

Atoms are not usually alone. Many atoms like to join together. When two or more atoms join together, they make a **molecule**.

> **[6.1]** H₂O.

Look at this one. Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom. Together they make a molecule of water. We write it as H two O.

One small glass of water has more water molecules than there are grains of sand on all the beaches of the Earth.
