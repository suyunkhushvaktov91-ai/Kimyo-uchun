In the centre of every atom there is a nucleus. The nucleus is very, very small. But it is also very heavy.

In fact, almost all the mass of the atom is in the nucleus. That brings us to our next word: mass. Mass is the amount of matter in an object. We measure mass in grams and kilograms.

The nucleus contains two kinds of particles. The first one is the proton. A proton has a positive charge. We show it with a plus sign.

The second one is the neutron. A neutron has no charge at all. It is neutral. Neutron, neutral. That is an easy way to remember it.

Now look around the nucleus. These very small, fast particles are electrons. An electron has a negative charge. We show it with a minus sign.

Electrons are very light. One electron is almost two thousand times lighter than one proton.

We have used the word "charge" a few times, so let's make it our next word. Charge is a property of a particle. It can be positive, negative, or zero.

And here is the important rule: opposite charges attract. The positive protons pull the negative electrons. That is why the electrons stay close to the nucleus.

And here is a surprising fact. Most of an atom is empty space.

Imagine an atom as big as a football stadium. The nucleus would be like a small pea in the middle of the field. And the electrons would be somewhere far away, around the seats at the top.
